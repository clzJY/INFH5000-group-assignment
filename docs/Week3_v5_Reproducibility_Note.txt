4. Engineering choices and metric definitions
Native physics step 0.02 s, five physics steps per 0.1 s decision; 600-decision budget. Forward-only motion; maximum tracking speed 3 m/s; normalised steering slew limit 1.5/s. Physical wheel braking and low-speed hold apply without overwriting robot pose or velocity.
Tracker lookahead: max(3 m, current speed × 1 s); minimum tracking speed 0.4 m/s; curvature radius parameter 3 m; goal approach distance 5 m.
Forecast: horizon 1.5 s, sampling 0.1 s, acceleration 1.5 m/s², braking 2 m/s², body buffer 0.25 m, release hold 0.2 s, speed candidates 3/2/1/0.5/0 m/s. Soft clearance 1 m and escape-improvement threshold 0.1 m. These are engineering assumptions, not measured human comfort limits.
Common static guard: 0.1 m buffer, 1 s horizon. RPP-inspired: proximity 1.5 m, projection 1 s, minimum progress speed 0.4 m/s. Distance yield slows between the common 0.25 m buffer and 1.5 m. The exact DWA steering offsets, command window and objective weights remain in the frozen JSON; it is a local Ackermann adaptation, not an official DWA implementation.
Only static TestObject footprints enter those planar checks; road/curb meshes are not covered. Native collision/out-of-road outcomes are retained. Forecast rollouts omit steering slew and detailed wheel transients and are sampled, so they cannot establish continuous collision avoidance.
Strict arrival requires native arrival AND endpoint distance ≤2 m; valid start-to-end and route lengths must both be ≥5 m. Pedestrian contact is crash_human OR crash_vehicle because native pedestrian bodies use a vehicle tag; other moving traffic is disabled. Contact-free arrival additionally excludes object/building/sidewalk flags.
WAIT time counts stop commands, not measured physical standstill or human delay. Near exposure is the observed navigation time fraction with sampled oriented-box gap <1 m, not the earlier circle proxy or measured discomfort. Episode termination changes its denominator. Displayed means use decimal round-half-up to two places; unrounded data are retained.

5. Independent numerical review and interpretation
This revision independently recomputed all 73,885 navigation decision steps across the 160 formal runs from existing raw JSONL. Goal distances, strict/native arrival, contacts, durations, stop-command time and sampled proximity agree with delivered episode records within 1e-7. The evidence audit and preservation checks are recorded centrally.
Forecast and zero-velocity ablation both have 16/20 strict and 13/20 contact-free arrivals, with three pedestrian-contact episodes. Forecast is 3.1625 s faster than RPP-inspired tracking on their 16 shared successes, but 0.28125 s slower than the zero-velocity ablation on those same successes. Differences from RPP alone cannot identify the contribution of measured pedestrian velocity.
Against DWA-style, six strict rescues minus four strict regressions give two additional strict arrivals (14→16). Contact-free arrivals change independently by one (12→13). A contact episode may still arrive; those measures are not interchangeable.
All five path-tracking methods time out in identifiers 305/306 at both crowds. Their buffer failures are diagnostic controller evidence, not proof that the physical passage is impassable. The proposed bounded detour is NOT implemented or evaluated. Its 120-run plan is future work.

6. Supplementary uncertainty, kept outside the main narrative
Existing outputs/classical/test/paired_cluster_intervals.json contains 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples of the ten scene identities (both crowds kept together), RNG seed 20261102. They are small-sample descriptive intervals, not rare-event safety guarantees. Identical paired arrival outcomes create degenerate [0,0] bootstrap intervals; this does not establish population equivalence. The proposal reports descriptive comparisons and makes no significance claim.
For forecast minus DWA, the existing 95% bootstrap interval is −0.30 to +0.50 for strict-arrival proportion and −0.30 to +0.40 for contact-free arrival. These wide intervals reinforce the exploratory interpretation; they do not justify a general performance claim.
