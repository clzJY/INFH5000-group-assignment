WHY THE REVISION
The previous circle/constant-heading shield could stop for paths the robot would
turn away from. Stopping also failed when a native pedestrian continued along its
preplanned path into the robot. Some failures had no shield interventions at all:
PPO asked for negative throttle, which means braking under the common forward-only
contract. Separately, the native >95% route-projection flag accepted points 5–17 m
from the intended endpoint in the old nontrivial successful baseline episodes.

CURRENT FOUR METHODS
baseline: fixed official PPO, original actions, common forward-only physics.
predictive: original circle / constant-velocity yield layer and steering unchanged.
route: local route-consistency steering/throttle correction plus a 3 m/s cruise
  governor with a curvature-dependent cap. Corrections, triggers and raw PPO
  actions are all retained. This uses the full simulator-provided planned route.
route_intent: the same route governor, plus 0/0.5/1/2/cruise candidate speed
  predictions along the upcoming route, with bounded acceleration/braking.
  Native 1.5 x 1 m ego and 1 x 1 m pedestrian boxes replace declared-radius
  assumptions in control. Disjoint planar footprint distances use exact Euclidean gaps;
  overlapping boxes use signed separating-axis penetration. Prediction is 1 s
  at 0.1 s intervals, with a 0.25 m geometric buffer. A stop is evaluated as a
  candidate, not assumed safe. No-buffer-feasible states are explicitly logged;
  least-predicted-overlap escape is a heuristic, not a safety guarantee.
  Pedestrian motion is constant-velocity prediction from current exact states,
  never access to their actual future trajectory.

OUTCOMES AND VALIDITY
All four methods must satisfy native arrival AND <=2 m endpoint distance.
Both raw native arrival and strict arrival remain in the logs. For the final
inspection, physical speed must remain below 0.1 m/s for 1 s within the goal.
No position/velocity teleportation is used for braking. A camera-only 90-degree
pan records the streetscape after stopping; RGB is never the policy input.

Before a run, route length and start-to-goal distance must both be >=5 m.
Invalid generated tasks are recorded, not replaced. Samples with speed >20 m/s
are numerical-anomaly flags and remain in primary counts. Pedestrian contacts
include native crash_vehicle in this verified pedestrian-only traffic setup;
all raw flags are retained. Any-contact-free goals are reported separately.
The reported near-time fraction retains the old circle proxy for comparability;
it is not native box clearance and is not a validated human-comfort measure.
